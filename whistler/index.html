Mon Jul 6 · two easy hikes

Sea to Sky Gondola + Shannon Falls

Howe Sound viewsWaterfallBuy tickets
  • Morning: leave Whistler after breakfast for Squamish. Ride the Sea to Sky Gondola; current summer posting shows June–Sept daily 9am–8pm, last ride down 9pm, but re-check the day before.
  • Hike 1: Spirit Loop (400m, beginner) plus Panorama Loop (1.3km, beginner) from the summit; add the Sky Pilot Suspension Bridge for the payoff.
  • Hike 2: Shannon Falls viewpoint trail before returning north. This is the easiest waterfall win of the trip.
  • Toddler/grandparent call: keep this as a scenic, low-grade day. Use the gondola lodge for lunch/bathrooms, then return for nap/reset.
  • Booking: buy Sea to Sky Gondola day tickets online in advance; no separate hiking permit found for the planned summit/frontcountry trails.

Tue Jul 7 · one moderate alpine hike

Whistler Blackcomb PEAK 2 PEAK day

Must-do gondolaAlpine lakes/viewsWeather dependent
  • Morning: bus #32/#33 to the mountain base or use Blackcomb lots 6–8 if driving. Upload via Whistler Village Gondola for access to Whistler Mountain, Blackcomb Mountain, and PEAK 2 PEAK.
  • Hike: choose one alpine loop that is open and snow-free: easy summit loops if grandparents/toddler need low effort, or Harmony Lake / High Note-style terrain for the adult subgroup if conditions are good.
  • Why this day matters: this is the big mountain sightseeing day: glaciers, alpine lakes, wildflowers, volcanic landscape, and the record-breaking PEAK 2 PEAK crossing.
  • Timing: official page currently says Summer Sightseeing is open daily 9:30am–5:00pm and PEAK 2 PEAK is daily starting June 13, 2026. Go early and keep the nap/reset protected.
  • Booking: buy Summer Alpine Experience tickets in advance. No BC Parks day-use pass applies to the lift-accessed Whistler Blackcomb trails.

Wed Jul 8 · two easy hikes

Brandywine Falls + Train Wreck / Cheakamus River

Waterfall dayRiver bridgeNo permit planned
  • Morning hike: Brandywine Falls viewpoint first for the 70m waterfall. If the group wants more distance, add the Lava Lake Trail section in the same park.
  • Lunch/reset: return to Whistler for lunch and downtime, or picnic if everyone is still fresh.
  • Afternoon hike: Whistler Train Wreck / Cheakamus River suspension bridge for an easy forest-and-river outing with a different feel from the gondola days.
  • Scale-down: if Wednesday is hot or the toddler is done, skip Train Wreck and do Lost Lake beach/Valley Trail instead.
  • Permit check: Brandywine Falls is not listed on the BC Parks day-use-pass page. Use normal day-use parking etiquette and verify any park advisories before leaving.

Thu Jul 9 · culture + waterfall hike

Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre + Nairn Falls

Indigenous cultureLil’wat routeWaterfall
  • Morning culture anchor: visit the Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre. The SLCC homepage lists regular hours Tuesday–Sunday 10am–5pm, closed Monday, and says Cultural Ambassadors share knowledge daily through hourly tours, songs, and stories.
  • Lunch: build in Thunderbird Café or Village lunch, then give the toddler a reset window.
  • Afternoon hike: drive north to Nairn Falls Park. BC Parks lists the Nairn Falls Trail as a 3km round trip route from day-use parking and notes part of it is a traditional route used by the Lil’wat Nation to access the falls and Mount Currie.
  • Closer backup: if the group wants less driving, do Lost Lake Park / Valley Trail instead. It gives lake time and bathrooms but less cultural context.
  • Optional museum backup: Audain Art Museum is a strong rain fallback on Thursday; its visit page currently lists Thu–Mon 11am–6pm, youth 18 and under free, and Indigenous Peoples free.

Advance permits, tickets, and reservations

Good news: the planned hikes avoid BC Parks day-use-pass trails.

The itinerary uses lift-accessed Whistler/Sea-to-Sky trails plus Shannon Falls, Brandywine Falls, Train Wreck, Lost Lake, and Nairn Falls. The BC Parks day-use-pass page lists Joffre Lakes, Garibaldi, and Golden Ears as summer pass parks; the planned waterfall/lake hikes are not in that pass bucket.

Indigenous culture + museums

Primary pick: SLCC on Thursday.

The Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre is the strongest fit because it is in Whistler, on the shared territory of the Sḵwx̱wú7mesh and Lil’wat7úl, and offers hourly tours, songs, stories, galleries, bannock/café, and a gift shop. I did not find a confirmed midweek Indigenous festival/show during Jul 6–9; SLCC’s homepage has current event/Instagram links, so check again before departure in case a special program appears.

Best cultural anchor

SLCC Thursday morning, then Nairn Falls in the afternoon to connect the cultural visit with a Lil’wat-area waterfall hike.

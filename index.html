Recommendation #1
Squamish + Whistler: the Sea-to-Sky family adventure.

Best overall choice

Why it wins: It is the most compelling answer to “why not just stay home?” Howe Sound, granite cliffs, alpine gondola views, Whistler Village, lakes, waterfalls, and Pemberton valley are meaningfully different from the Eastside backyard.

Open lodging listing

Day 1 · Arrive

Border crossing, Sea-to-Sky drive, optional Shannon Falls reset, Squamish groceries, early bedtime.

Day 2 · Gondola

Sea to Sky Gondola, summit bridge/views, lunch, nap, downtown Squamish stroll.

Day 3 · Lake day

Alice Lake picnic, shore play, short trails, nap, easy dinner.

Day 4 · Whistler

Whistler Village, Olympic Plaza, optional Peak 2 Peak if clear, Lost Lake/Rainbow Park.

Day 5 · Pemberton

Nairn Falls with carrier or Brandywine Falls fallback; final Squamish dinner.

Day 6 · Home

Pack early, check DriveBC/border waits, return before Vancouver traffic stacks up.

Family upside: short high-reward activities; grocery access; pack-and-play confirmed; enough variety that bad weather or toddler fatigue does not collapse the trip.
Decision caveat: border + Vancouver + Sea-to-Sky traffic are real. This works best with early departures and no Friday-afternoon optimism.
Recommendation #2
North Vancouver: city, harbor, rainforest, easy backups.

Easiest Canada trip

Why it works: North Vancouver makes the international trip feel real without making every day a car project: Shipyards, Stanley Park, Lynn Canyon, Grouse Mountain, SeaBus, Granville Island, restaurants, and full grocery access.

Day 1 · Arrive

Border crossing, North Van groceries, Lower Lonsdale / Shipyards waterfront stroller walk.

Day 2 · Stanley Park

Seawall, playground, totem poles, picnic; Aquarium as weather backup.

Day 3 · Rainforest

Lynn Canyon with carrier; Maplewood Farm for toddler-forward outing.

Day 4 · Mountain

Grouse Mountain Skyride or Capilano; nap; optional SeaBus ride.

Day 5 · Market day

Granville Island Public Market + Kids Market; optional Queen Elizabeth Park.

Day 6 · Home

Choose departure around border waits; avoid Friday late afternoon if possible.

Family upside: lowest operational risk, most stroller-compatible, best restaurants/groceries, and strongest rainy-day coverage.
Decision caveat: less “epic nature vacation” than Squamish/Whistler. Pick this if family consensus values ease and variety over alpine drama.
Recommendation #3
Sooke + Victoria: harbor, tidepools, ferry adventure.

Best coastal/ferry option

Why it works: Sooke adds marina life, rugged beaches, tidepools, Sooke Basin, and Victoria’s Inner Harbour. It is more of a full coastal vacation than a simple hiking base.

Aylard Farm / East Sooke short coastal wandering; picnic; tidepool time.

Inner Harbour, Fisherman’s Wharf, Beacon Hill Park, optional museum/garden.

Day 5 · Beach

French Beach for easier access or Sandcut with carrier; final marina dinner.

Family upside: water, ferry, beach, harbor, and Victoria in one package; lodging has strong space and laundry practicality.
Decision caveat: ferry + border + island driving makes this the hardest logistics of the top three. Also more overlap with prior island/coastal trips.

Travel-site layout patterns used

  • Hero promise first: sell the feeling before details.
  • Ranked cards: make comparison fast for a family group.
  • Proof blocks: dates, price, beds, toddler fit, and logistics are visible above the day-by-day plan.
  • Day-by-day rhythm: each day has one main outing, nap/reset space, and an easy evening.
  • Caveats are explicit: border/ferry/traffic risks are surfaced before anyone emotionally commits.

Important caveats

  • Displayed lodging totals were live at research time and can change.
  • Final checkout may add taxes or change cancellation terms.
  • Confirm pack-and-play floor space with host before booking.
  • For Canada, everyone should have valid border documents, including toddler.
  • Check CBSA/WSDOT border waits, DriveBC, and BC Ferries before travel.

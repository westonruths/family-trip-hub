Mon Jul 6 · two easy hikes

Sea to Sky Gondola + Shannon Falls

Howe Sound viewsWaterfallBuy tickets
  • Morning: leave Whistler after breakfast for Squamish. Ride the Sea to Sky Gondola; plan around June–Sept daily service, roughly 9am–8pm with late download service; confirm the day-before operating notice before leaving.
  • Hike 1: Spirit Loop (400m, beginner) plus Panorama Loop (1.3km, beginner) from the summit; add the Sky Pilot Suspension Bridge for the payoff.
  • Hike 2: Shannon Falls viewpoint trail before returning north. This is the easiest waterfall win of the trip.
  • Toddler/grandparent call: keep this as a scenic, low-grade day. Summit Lodge, Basecamp, viewing areas, and the larger-path summit trails are the most stroller-friendly parts; bring the carrier for roots/steps.
  • Tickets + parking: budget from about $73.95/adult, $68.95/senior, $43.95/youth, $29.95/child 6–12, children 5 and under free, or a family ticket from about $165.95. No ride-time reservation is needed for standard gondola tickets; buy online to save. Parking is $10 + taxes/day.
  • Permit check: no separate hiking permit is planned for the summit/frontcountry trails.

Tue Jul 7 · one moderate alpine hike

Whistler Blackcomb PEAK 2 PEAK day

Must-do gondolaAlpine lakes/viewsWeather dependent
  • Morning: bus #32/#33 to the mountain base or use Blackcomb lots 6–8 if driving. Upload via Whistler Village Gondola for access to Whistler Mountain, Blackcomb Mountain, and PEAK 2 PEAK.
  • Hike: pick from the official 2026 hiking map: Spearhead Loop (1km / 0.6mi, about 60 min, 20m gain, easy) for the whole group; Harmony Lake Loop (1.9km / 1.2mi, about 90 min, 120m gain) if the carrier/grandparent group wants a moderate lake loop.
  • Why this day matters: this is the big mountain sightseeing day: glaciers, alpine lakes, wildflowers, volcanic landscape, and the record-breaking PEAK 2 PEAK crossing.
  • Timing: use the posted summer sightseeing window: Whistler Village Gondola and Blackcomb Gondola 9:30am–5pm, PEAK 2 PEAK 10am–5pm, last recommended upload 4:30pm, and last PEAK 2 PEAK round trip 4:45pm. Go early and keep the nap/reset protected.
  • Tickets + parking: budget about $110/adult, $100/senior or teen, $60/child 7–12, and free for tots 3–6 for the Summer Alpine 1-day ticket. Village Day Lots 1–5 are paid 8am–5pm year-round; Day Lots 1–3 are about $4/hr or $21/day and Day Lots 4–5 are about $3.50/hr or $13/day.
  • Permit check: no BC Parks day-use pass applies to the lift-accessed Whistler Blackcomb trails.

Wed Jul 8 · must-do crash train car hike

Whistler Train Wreck + Brandywine Falls

Crash train carsRiver bridgeWaterfall add-onNo permit planned
  • Morning must-do: Whistler Train Wreck first, so the crash train car experience is protected. VancouverTrails describes it as an easy ~2km hike following the Sea to Sky Trail, crossing the Cheakamus River suspension bridge, then reaching the painted boxcars that have sat in the forest since 1956.
  • Logistics: use the Jane Lakes Road / Train Wreck parking approach near Function Junction; expect roots and a short steep downhill by the bridge, so bring the carrier for Aria and keep toddlers close.
  • Lunch/reset: return to Whistler for lunch and downtime, or picnic if everyone is still fresh.
  • Second easy hike: Brandywine Falls viewpoint for the 70m waterfall. If the group wants more distance, add the Lava Lake Trail section in the same park.
  • Scale-down: if Wednesday is hot or the toddler is done, skip Train Wreck and do Lost Lake beach/Valley Trail instead.
  • Waterfall/lake alternate: if the family wants to stay closer to Whistler, swap this day to Lost Lake Nature Trail plus Rainbow Falls Loop. Lost Lake parking is closed May 15–Sept 15 and is pickup/drop-off only; use the free shuttle, walk, bike, or Valley Trail. The free Lost Lake shuttle is posted for June 27–Sept 7, 2026, 11am–7pm.
  • Permit check: Brandywine Falls is not listed on the BC Parks day-use-pass page. Use normal day-use parking etiquette and verify any park advisories before leaving.

Thu Jul 9 · culture + waterfall hike

Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre + Nairn Falls

Indigenous cultureLil’wat routeWaterfall
  • Morning culture anchor: visit the Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre. SLCC is closed Monday but open Tuesday–Sunday 10am–5pm during this window. The July programming page lists What We Treasure tours at 10am, 12pm, 1pm, 2pm, and 4pm, plus a seasonal Interpretive Forest Walk at 3pm.
  • Tickets: current SLCC admission is adult $25, child 6–18 $12, children 5 and under free, family pass $50 plus GST, and Indigenous Peoples free with valid Status Card.
  • Lunch: build in Thunderbird Café or Village lunch, then give the toddler a reset window.
  • Afternoon hike: drive north to Nairn Falls Park. BC Parks lists the Nairn Falls Trail as a 3km round trip route from day-use parking and notes part of it is a traditional route used by the Lil’wat Nation to access the falls and Mount Currie.
  • Easy lake add-on: if the group has energy after Nairn, add One Mile Lake in Pemberton, an easy roughly 1.45–1.6km lake loop with washrooms and beach access.
  • Closer backup: if the group wants less driving, do Lost Lake Park / Valley Trail instead. It gives lake time and bathrooms but less cultural context.
  • Optional museum backup: Audain Art Museum is a strong rain fallback on Thursday; plan for Thu–Mon 11am–6pm hours; youth 18 and under and Indigenous Peoples are free.

Indigenous culture + museums

Primary pick: SLCC on Thursday.

The Squamish Lil’wat Cultural Centre is the strongest fit because it is in Whistler, on the shared territory of the Sḵwx̱wú7mesh and Lil’wat7úl, and offers tours, songs, stories, galleries, bannock/café, and a gift shop. The in-window special event is the SLCC Spo7ez Artist Market on arrival day, Sunday Jul 5, 10am–4pm, if arrival timing allows. For the full activity days, use the daily SLCC cultural programming as the reliable culture anchor.

Best cultural anchor

SLCC Thursday morning, then Nairn Falls in the afternoon to connect the cultural visit with a Lil’wat-area waterfall hike.
